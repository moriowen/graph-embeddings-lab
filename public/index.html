Experiment write-up · graph embeddings for recommendation

The loss function, not the random walk, separates DeepWalk from node2vec

I ran both algorithms on Wikipedia link graphs of 222 and 4,589 articles to recommend related articles. Out of the box, DeepWalk wins on the large graph. But its code quietly trains two losses at once. Give both algorithms the same loss and they tie there, and a one-line heuristic beats them both at predicting links.

What the experiment shows

  1. The repo comparison measures the wrong thing.DeepWalk's code passes hs=1 and leaves gensim's negative=5 on, so it trains both losses. node2vec trains negative sampling only. That accounts for most of the gap between them on the large graph.
  2. Walk bias is real but tiny.On the large graph, tuning p and q stays positive on all three edge splits (), about a tenth of the loss effect. On the small graph its sign changes across splits, so I make no claim there.
  3. The two ways to measure quality disagree.Adamic-Adar finds more held-out links on both graphs. Embeddings find more articles on the query's topic: lift against on the large graph. "Better" depends on which you mean.
  4. Walks don't reach further here.The usual case for embeddings is finding nodes beyond two hops. But of the large graph's test edges are already within two hops, and embeddings spend of their slots there anyway.
  5. Hubs drive the heuristic, not the embeddings.Adamic-Adar gives of its slots to the top 1% of articles and never recommends of all articles. DeepWalk gives hubs and recommends every article at least once.
  6. One list proves very little.Two seeds of the same algorithm share only of a top-5 list on average, not much more than the two algorithms share with each other (). Conclusions here come from all-node metrics, not from examples that look good.

Is it the walk or the loss?

Reading across a group compares the walks. Reading between groups compares the losses, and that is where the difference is.

The confidence intervals resample query nodes, which share one graph and so aren't truly independent, so they run optimistic. Seed t is the gap in units of its standard error across seeds. Is it just too few negatives?
Partly. node2vec walks with p=q=1, NDCG@20. Runs with more negatives took much longer to train.

"Embedding distance" hides a choice

Same vectors, different metric: lists share only about half their members, and the winner changes.

DeepWalk vectors under three distance metrics
5 seeds. I expected dot product to favour high-norm hubs. The reverse happens, because vector norm falls as degree rises.

Treat this as a lead, not a result. On the large graph Euclidean passes Adamic-Adar on both measures, the only time an embedding does. But I found it after looking at test scores, it doesn't hold on the small graph, and the metric was never selected on validation.

What I still don't know

  • Why HS beats NS. It holds on every graph where I ran it. More negatives close part of the gap on the large graph and most of it on the small one, but the rest isn't established.
  • The small graph's same-loss gap. Running the same-loss DeepWalk row on two more splits would settle it.
  • The cause of the lift gap. It may be mostly hub avoidance. Re-scoring Adamic-Adar's lift with hubs removed would test that.
  • Whether Euclidean generalises. It needs selection on validation and more graphs.
  • Word2vec's leftover scaling factor. A cache-miss count would settle it.
  • Whether a link is a good recommendation. A held-out link is what an editor chose to link. Wikispeedia's human navigation paths would be a better signal to test against.

Setup

Graphs
The small Wikipedia graph is nested inside the large one, so the same articles can be queried on both.
  • Code. phanein/deepwalk at 6e6dff2 and eliorc/node2vec at 872af47, on gensim 4.3.3, with a small patch for import failures and seeding. Neither imports as shipped on Python 3.11.
  • Evaluation. A seeded spanning tree is protected, then 20% of edges are held out for test and 10% for validation, before any walk. Candidates exclude the query and known edges. NDCG@20 was fixed as the primary metric before results.
  • Controls. Random, popularity, common neighbours and Adamic-Adar baselines; both walks crossed with three losses; p and q chosen on validation only, then re-run with fresh seeds.
  • Limits. Lift isn't comparable between the two Wikipedia graphs because their label schemes differ. Sweeps and hub tests use 3 seeds, other rows 5. Quality runs used two CPU models. The paper reports classifier AUC, so its result and this ranking result aren't the same test.
  • Bugs caught. The tuned row first averaged in its own selection seeds, and a regex silently dropped the ego-Facebook runs. Both showed up because a number looked wrong.
  • AI use. Built with Claude Code, which wrote the code and drafted the analysis. No model output is treated as ground truth: every number here comes from held-out edges and dataset labels.
